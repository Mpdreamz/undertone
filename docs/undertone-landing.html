A macOS menu bar app

Hide the notch. Keep the colour.

Undertone hides the notch and the screen's borders in black, and fills the rest with beautiful generated backgrounds: gradients and shapes that follow your light or dark theme and change on a schedule.

Notch and borders

The notch, gone. The corners, black.

A black strip as tall as the menu bar swallows the notch, so the menu bar reads as bezel and the screen starts below it. Rounded corners put black behind every window tiled into a corner, so the picture never peeks out round an app's own rounded corner.

  • The strip is drawn only on displays with a notch.
  • The corners curve like macOS's own windows, sized so every window's corner meets black.
Lock screen motion

It moves while you're away.

While the Mac is locked, your picture drifts: the curtains sway, the waves roll, the glows wander. Unlock, and it slows to a stop on your desktop.

  • Your picture, in its own style, tonality and layout, not a stock video.
  • Drawn in the background after each shuffle, played by the Mac's video hardware.
  • Your pace: calm, normal or lively; subtle or strong; and it comes to rest with a glide, an ease or a spring.
  • One switch in the menu, and Undertone as your screen saver.
Eleven styles

Gradients, and a lot of shapes.

Glows, marbling, layered waves, lit spheres, rings, dunes, topographic lines, silk, aurora, a bauhaus grid and a lava lamp. Every picture is generated: each tile below is shuffling, its style locked, its tonality and layout new every time. Lock a style, or let every shuffle pick.

Follows your theme

Light mode, dark mode, eight tonalities.

Each tonality has a dark half and a light half. Switch appearance and the picture switches with it: the same shapes, the other colours. Lock a tonality, or let it change on every shuffle, independently of the style.

Seven effects

Print, glass and film.

Dither, halftone, reeded glass, bloom, a light leak, paper and posterize, over any style in any tonality. Lock one, leave them off, or let every shuffle pick a new one.

On a schedule

A new background every day. Or hour.

Shuffle by hand, from a hotkey, or let Undertone pick a new picture every hour, every three hours or every day, and on login and wake. Style and tonality are separate choices: lock either, and only the other one changes.

  • Theme switch: the same picture, redrawn from the other half of its tonality.
  • Drawn on your Mac at each display's full resolution, once, then reused.
  • No permissions to grant.

Spaces

A picture per space, one tonality.

Every space gets its own picture from its own seed, in the same style and colours, so spaces are siblings and you can tell them apart. Or show the same one everywhere.

Grain and a title

Film grain, and the machine's name.

A fine grain takes the plastic sheen off a gradient and hides banding. A centred title, off until you want it, tells machines apart in a screen share: {computer}, {user}, {os} and {host} are filled in.

One menu

Everything a click away.

Shuffle, lock a style, a tonality or an effect, a picture per space, automatic shuffles every hour, three hours or day, grain, the notch, corners and a title: all in the menu bar item, with a settings window for the text and the sliders.

  • undertone shuffle for a hotkey.
  • undertone render draws any picture to a file, without the app.
  • Updates itself, signed and notarized.

Questions

Does it need any permissions?

No. Setting the desktop picture needs none, and Undertone asks for nothing else.

Does it follow dark mode on its own?

Yes. When the system switches between light and dark, including on a schedule, Undertone redraws the same picture from the other half of its tonality.

Does it cost battery?

A picture is drawn once, in about half a second, when you shuffle or change a setting, and reused after that. The checks compare a file name. With lock screen motion on, each new picture is also drawn as a movie, a few minutes in the background on two cores; playing it is the Mac's video hardware.

Which Macs?

Apple Silicon Macs on macOS 15 or later.

Is it free?

Yes, and open source under the MIT licence.
